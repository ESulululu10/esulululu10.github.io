Social web inspired video for nursing education

Social Web-Inspired Video to Support Student Attainment of AACN Essentials — short form video in the format students already watch, produced through an AI assisted pipeline, for an undergraduate nursing course.

Status
Manuscript in preparation
Role
Designed and built the production pipeline
Where
School of Nursing, University of Michigan Flint · PI: Dr. Beverly Dabney
Since
January 2026

The premise

Nursing programs are expected to show that students attain the competencies set out in the AACN Essentials. The material that carries those competencies is usually built for a lecture slot — long, static, and in a format students do not otherwise choose to watch. The premise of this work is that the short, social web video format students already use every day can carry that content instead, and that producing it should not cost an instructor a week per topic.

What I built

An AI assisted production pipeline that takes a topic and carries it through to a video sitting in the course:

  • script generation with a language model, written to the competency the video has to serve;
  • image prompting for the visuals the script calls for;
  • assembly into a finished short video with a presenter;
  • delivery through the course’s learning management system, so the videos land where students already are.

The point of the pipeline is throughput. A production route that an instructor can actually run is what makes it possible to cover a competency set at all, rather than producing two polished videos and stopping.

The Evidence Elevator

One piece of this is mine as a design contribution rather than an engineering one: a visual device I designed for teaching the hierarchy of evidence, moving from expert opinion at the bottom up to meta analysis at the top. It gives students a single spatial image to hang the hierarchy on — the thing that is hard to convey in a bulleted list and easy to convey in fifteen seconds of video.

Why this sits next to my robotics work. It is the same question in a different medium: a person with domain expertise but no production or programming background needs to express something, and a model does the composition while they keep control of the content. In my thesis work the output is robot motion; here it is instructional video. What stays constant is who is authoring and who is approving.
