About

I am a master’s student in Computer Science at the University of Michigan–Flint, where I work with Dr. Ananya Ipsita in PLAYLab on end-user authoring for human–multi-robot interaction. My thesis work grew out of a concrete failure in my earlier system: expressive robot behaviors had to be re-engineered by hand for every new scenario. So I built a system where novices author the motions, by sketching or by physical demonstration, and an LLM reuses that motion library across scenarios — with the person reviewing and editing the plan before any robot moves.

I care about the human side of that loop: what people can author, what they can read off a robot’s motion, and where they need to intervene when a model proposes something wrong. I also mentor an undergraduate researcher on accessible AR authoring through UM–Flint’s NSF REU Site in Digital Accessibility. I am applying to PhD programs in HCI and HRI for Fall 2027 entry.

Research

One question, three systems

Each project below answers a piece of the same question: how does a person who is not a roboticist tell a team of robots what to do, and know what they are doing? Each page states the problem, the system, the study, and what it changed about the next project.

M.S. thesis HRI 2027 · under review

Authoring motion for robot teams

How can someone with no robotics background author expressive motion for a robot team — and reuse it when the scenario changes?

Novices author the motions themselves — either by sketching the path they want, or by demonstrating the motion physically. A language model then composes those motions into a plan for a scenario described in plain English, and the person reviews and edits that plan before any robot moves. I built the whole stack end to end — the robots, the localization and control server, both authoring interfaces and the planner — and evaluated it in a counterbalanced within-subjects study comparing the two ways of authoring.

Described in general terms while the paper is under review. Full detail is in my CV.

ExpressBot

Can non-experts tell what a robot is doing from its motion alone?

A three-robot fleet (about $30 per robot) with seven motion primitives — Searching, Delivering, Urgent and others — navigating by camera-based ArUco tracking. In a user study (N = 11), non-experts could read robot state from motion alone (SUS ≈ 70). Two failures drove the next project: the behaviors were locked to a single scenario, and Urgent and Searching were regularly confused for each other.

EventMakAR

Can people who do not program author AR stories that drive physical robots?

A web-based authoring tool for creating AR stories with five robot characters, with no programming involved. It was deployed live at the Super Friday workshop at UM–Flint, where students authored and ran their own AR stories with physical robots — the first time this authoring model was put in front of users outside the lab.
